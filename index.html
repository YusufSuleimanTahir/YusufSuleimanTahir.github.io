About me

Research that ships.

I hold a Ph.D. from Universiti Sains Malaysia, where I specialised in finger vein recognition and presentation attack detection. I design compact networks shaped to the geometry of vein images, and I evaluate them rigorously, including a new multi-material spoof database and subject-disjoint testing that shows where supervised anti-spoofing breaks down.

I also build what I research. With CyberSecurity Malaysia, I helped develop multimodal face, fingerprint, iris and vein recognition systems and a finger vein anti-spoofing application. I have also taught at university level in Nigeria and Cyprus.

Doctoral research
PhD · core thesis work

Finger vein presentation attack detection

Lightweight networks that separate genuine finger vein images from spoofs made of different materials, evaluated with ISO/IEC 30107-3 metrics. A multi-material study shows that supervised methods struggle with spoof materials they were not trained on, which points to anomaly detection, one-class and domain-adaptation formulations as the way forward.

  • 0.00% ACER on IDIAP and SCUT-SFVD (five seeds), with 488K parameters (vs. 1.1M–3.5M for compared methods)
  • Built FVPAD-USM: 720 genuine and 2,160 spoof images from glossy photo paper, high-quality paper and an AMOLED display (available on request)
  • Showed that unseen-material APCER reaches 57–99%, and traced the difficulty of display spoofs to their self-emitted light mimicking NIR transillumination
  • Turned into a Windows anti-spoofing app connected to a commercial finger vein scanner through its SDK
  • International Journal of Biometrics (in press, 2026)

PhD · recognition

FV-EffResNet

A compact CNN built around the EffRes block: pointwise and depthwise convolution with asymmetric (n×1)(1×m) kernels matched to elongated vein images.

  • 98.5–99.5% Rank-1 on FV-USM, MMCBNU_6000, SDUMLA-HMT and NUPT-FPV
  • 1.32M parameters (baseline: 5.38M)
  • PeerJ Computer Science, 2024
